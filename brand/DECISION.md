# Brand decision: Corridor Ledger

Status: accepted by the brand strategist; the design evaluator judges it on the built product.

## Decision

Corridor Ledger: a depot-slip identity where the route/tanker/price input is a single stamped fuel-log strip, one dominant corridor green-black, one signal amber accent, warm paper neutrals, and a display/body/mono type pairing with real

## Alternatives rejected

- **Pipeline-dream brand**: It borrows the $660m pipeline story as a hero and implies the infrastructure problem is being solved. The research says the gap is severe and long-standing, and the research itself was rejected by the StartupTribunal AI Tribunal and
- **Generic logistics dashboard**: Centred gradient hero, three identical emoji feature cards, Inter with no contrast. It is interchangeable with any other SaaS tool and fails the evaluator's test of a brand a design-literate visitor would remember a day later.
- **Policy-report style**: It talks to analysts about the corridor bottleneck, not to the team entering numbers. The audience is logistics and real-estate teams doing the run, so a report voice misplaces the product and makes the tool feel like a

## Risks

- Alarmism: leaning on the supply-disruption and inflated-price facts to frighten the user instead of helping them plan
- Implied cloud sync: any copy or icon that suggests the data leaves the device contradicts the offline-first, on-device constraint
- Invented figures: any number in copy that is not in the brief (savings, speedups, percentages) is a claim the product cannot back
- Over-relying on the rejected-research status: the caveats must be stated once, plainly, not repeated as a defensive watermark on every screen

## Reversal

Revisit this decision when: If the product stops being a route/capacity/price input tool and becomes a macro research briefing about the Ethiopian fuel corridor, the ledger identity is wrong and a report identity is right.

Product: Corridor Route.
